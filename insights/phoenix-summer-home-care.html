How do you take care of a Phoenix home through the summer?

Glasshouse CollectiveSeptember 9, 20264 min read

A Phoenix home needs seasonal attention in ways homes in milder climates do not. Heat, monsoon storms and intense sun accelerate wear on air conditioning, roofing, irrigation and exterior finishes. The maintenance below is what protects both your comfort in July and your sale price later.

What should you do before summer arrives?

Service the air conditioning in spring, before the first stretch of extreme heat. Technicians book out quickly once temperatures climb, and a system that struggles in June will fail in July.

Change filters on schedule through the season rather than by memory. Restricted airflow makes a healthy system work harder and shortens its life, which is an expensive way to save a small amount.

How do you prepare for monsoon season?

Monsoon storms bring high wind, blowing dust and sudden heavy rain. Before the season, walk the property and address anything that can become airborne, including loose patio furniture, umbrellas and shade sails.

Trim trees away from the roofline and inspect for limbs that will not survive a microburst. Clear drainage paths and scuppers so that water leaves the roof and moves away from the foundation rather than pooling against it.

What does the sun do to a Valley home?

Ultraviolet exposure degrades exterior paint, sealants, weather stripping and pool equipment faster here than in most of the country. South and west elevations take the worst of it.

Check window and door seals annually. Failed weather stripping is a small repair that quietly raises cooling costs all summer. Exterior paint on the sun-facing sides will need attention sooner than the shaded elevations, and touching those up is cheaper than repainting the whole house later.

How should you handle irrigation and landscaping?

Adjust irrigation timers seasonally rather than setting them once. Overwatering in the cooler months and underwatering in June are both common and both damaging.

Inspect drip emitters for clogs and breaks. A failed emitter can kill an established tree quietly over a few weeks in summer, and a mature tree is one of the more valuable features on a Valley lot.

Does deferred maintenance affect your sale price?

Yes, and usually by more than the repair would have cost. Buyers discount for uncertainty. An air conditioning system at the end of its life, visible roof wear or a struggling landscape all invite a repair request after inspection, and those negotiations rarely land in the seller's favor.

When we prepare a home for market, the first conversation is about which items are worth addressing beforehand and which are better handled as a credit. Not every home needs work before listing. But the decision should be made deliberately rather than discovered during the inspection period.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

When should you service air conditioning in Phoenix?

Schedule service in spring, before sustained high temperatures arrive. Availability tightens sharply once the heat sets in, and a marginal system is far more likely to fail under peak load.

When is monsoon season in Arizona?

The Arizona monsoon season officially runs from June 15 through September 30, bringing high winds, blowing dust and heavy localized rain.

What maintenance matters most before selling a Phoenix home?

Air conditioning condition, roof condition and functioning irrigation tend to matter most, because they are the items buyers ask about and inspectors flag. Ask your agent which repairs are worth making before listing and which are better handled as a credit at closing.
